  Today began with a fun team-building activity where all of the participants in the program played Taboo. My team ended up winning, which made the activity even more enjoyable. It was a good reminder that communication sometimes requires adjusting to your audience.

  After the activity, I began working on the first phase of our methodology, which focuses on building and evaluating reference models. This marked the start of the development portion of our project and gave me the opportunity to apply concepts that we have been discussing throughout the program.

  I implemented and tested logistic regression and XGBoost models on our dataset. These models were evaluated across different data layers, including genotype, gene expression, methylation, and a combined dataset. I also established the majority-class baseline, which achieved an accuracy of 0.550. This baseline provides a reference point for evaluating whether more advanced models are actually learning useful patterns from the data.

  One of the most interesting findings was that the gene expression data produced the strongest results, with logistic regression achieving the highest performance among the models I tested. Seeing the differences in performance across data types helped me better understand how the choice of data can significantly impact model outcomes.
