what_i_learned: |
  Today we focused on making sure our datasets are clean and reliable before continuing with model development. We compared different patient datasets to understand how similar they are and whether they can be safely combined or analyzed together.

  A major part of the day involved checking for duplicate entries across the data. Identifying duplicates is important because repeated samples can distort results and make models seem more accurate than they actually are. We worked through the data carefully to ensure that each patient record was unique and properly represented.

  We also discussed potential sources of data leakage. This included thinking about situations where information from the test set might accidentally appear in the training set, which could falsely improve model performance. Catching these issues early is important to make sure our results are valid and trustworthy.

  Overall, this step helped us better understand the structure of our data and gave us more confidence moving forward with analysis and modeling.

blockers: |
  One challenge today was carefully tracing how data was merged and processed to ensure there were no hidden duplicates or overlaps between datasets. It took time to think through how leakage could happen and verify that our pipeline avoids it.

reflection: |
  Today reinforced how important data integrity is in research and machine learning projects. Even small issues like duplicates or leakage can significantly impact results, so taking time to carefully check the data was necessary.

  I also learned that good modeling starts long before training a model. Understanding the structure, relationships, and potential issues in the data is just as important as the models themselves.

  Overall, today helped strengthen my attention to detail and gave me more confidence in the reliability of our project moving forward.
